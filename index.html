    About

    Hi, I'm Rizul.

    How do systems process information, and where do they break down?

    I'm an undergraduate at the University of Toronto studying Neuroscience, Computer Science, and Statistics! I’m interested in a shared question across those fields: how do systems process information, and where do they break down?

    I build software and simulations, write about cognitive science and AI, and create learning resources tailored to individual students. My work ranges from reinforcement-learning transit models to a campus innovation game and research on AI-assisted scholarship.

    I’m most energized by projects where technical systems meet human needs: making complex information clearer, learning more engaging, or technology more accountable!

        The AI Threat to Academic Credibility

        Year-one paper on how personalization, confirmation bias, and the availability heuristic can interact in AI-assisted scholarship. It argues for AI literacy, transparent systems, and verification rather than uncritical reliance.

        BIO152 · Scientific literacy op-ed

        Can Machines Ever See Like Us?

        Artificial neural networks capture parts of the visual cortex’s hierarchy, but robustness, feedback, and out-of-distribution generalization remain meaningful gaps. The evidence suggests functional resemblance, not a complete biological replica.
